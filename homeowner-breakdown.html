The short version

Three things you don't deal with

One — the fees

Zero on your side

No commission. No closing costs on your end. I get paid by the buyer at closing.

On the market, between the agents and the seller's side of closing, you're giving up 7–8%. On a million-dollar house that's $70,000–$80,000. That's not a discount I'm offering — it's money that never leaves your pocket.

Two — the listing

Nobody has to know

No open houses. No photographer in your bedroom. No sign in the yard telling the whole street your business.

Sometimes it's one guy, one visit, twenty minutes — and he doesn't even have to go inside.

Three — the deadline

You pick the timeline

Close in thirty days if you've already got the next place. Or next spring, after your kids graduate.

Need to stay in the house a few months after closing while you figure out where you're going? That's normal here. Nobody's standing outside with boxes.

If that sounds too good — fair

Here's the actual mechanics

It's me and a short list of builders I talk to. They tell me what they're hunting, I go find it, and they pay me at closing. That's it.

I'd be out of business fast if I brought them bad deals — so I'm not going to waste your time with a lowball either.

And if squeezing out the absolute highest number matters more to you than any of this — put it on the market and go get every dollar you can. Genuinely. This isn't for everybody.

Straight answers

The questions everybody asks first

What does this cost me?

Nothing. No commission, and no closing costs on your side. I'm paid by the buyer at closing, not by you.

Can you just tell me a number from my address?

No — and be careful with anyone who does. What a builder pays depends on how much of the lot they can actually build on, and I can't see that from the street.

Ten minutes on the phone and I can tell you something real.

Am I committing to anything by texting you?

No. You're not signing anything. If it's not for you, tell me no and we're done. Your house, your call.

I don't know where I'd move yet. Is it too early to talk?

No — that's kind of the point of talking early. Most people don't have it figured out. You can close in thirty days, or next spring, and staying in the house for a few months after closing is normal here.

Will my neighbors find out I'm selling?

Not from me. There's no listing, no sign, no open house, and no photos online. Sometimes it's one visit, twenty minutes, and he doesn't even go inside.

Is this the best way to sell?

Not always, and I'll say so. If getting the absolute highest number matters more to you than the fees, the privacy, and the timeline — list it on the market and go get every dollar. This isn't for everybody.

What do I send in the first text?

Just your name and your address. That's enough to start. We'll set up ten minutes on the phone from there.
